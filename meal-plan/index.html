Three Meals, Weighed

The same three meals every day — no deciding, no rotating templates. Every number below is grams on the scale, and every meat weight is cooked weight, because that's what actually goes in the Tupperware. Weigh it once, and the day lands on target without any arithmetic.

01

Tare the container. Tupperware on the scale, press tare, then add food. The lid stays off.

02

Cooked weight, always. Meat and rice are weighed after cooking, out of the pan, not raw.

03

Sauce goes on the scale too. Pour the vinaigrette straight into the cup on the scale — it carries real fat, so eyeballing it is what drifts.

04

Close enough is close enough. Within 5 g is on target. Don't chase the last gram.

Dinner Protein Swaps

Breakfast and the lunch box never change. Dinner is the one slot that moves — and only two numbers move with it: how much meat, and how much sauce. Leaner cuts need more sauce to carry the fat; 80/20 ground beef needs almost none.

Rice and salad stay at the dinner amounts on the card — 215 g rice for Mike, 130 g for Janna, 100 g salad each — with one exception: on chicken nights the garlic-honey butter brings its own honey, so drop the rice to 180 g / 100 g to stay on carbs. Chicken is the least efficient cut here — it takes about 70 g of added fat to reach the same place a flap steak reaches on 32 g, which is why it's a once-a-week slot, not a staple.

Backup dressing · not in the gram math
03

Maple-Butter

Melted butter + maple syrup + pinch salt.

Treat · not in the gram math
04

Mustard Vinaigrette

200 g olive oil + 65 g balsamic + 20 g French mustard + 15 g water, garlic, salt, pepper. Makes 300 g.

The house dressing · 67% fat

The two batch recipes are written in grams on purpose: the sauce is a real part of the fat target, not a garnish, so the ratio has to be fixed for the weighed pours to mean anything. Whisk them exactly as written, keep each in its own squeeze bottle, and every gram poured onto the scale lands where the plan says. Vinaigrette: 6.7 g fat per 10 g poured. Garlic-honey butter: 5.8 g fat and 2.3 g carbs per 10 g.

Quick Tips

The plan didn't fail before because it was wrong — it failed because it asked for a decision at every meal. These are the habits that keep it from asking.

—

Build the boxes Sunday or the week doesn't happen. Ten containers, weighed, lids on. Weighing at 6 a.m. is the version that gets skipped.

—

Breakfast never changes. Four eggs, a scoop of beef, a banana, honey — there is nothing to decide before coffee.

—

Lunch never changes either. The only thing that moves all week is which meat goes on the dinner plate.

—

80/20 ground beef only — the fat is the point, don't drain it. The gram math assumes the fat stays in the pan with the meat.

—

Weigh the sauce. It is 20–30% of the day's fat. A free-hand pour is the single biggest source of drift in this whole plan.

—

Ground beef is always the answer to a night that fell apart — 232 g / 202 g, no sauce, done.

—

Flap steak stays fresh — it doesn't reheat well, and it's a 7-minute sear. Wednesday's only top-up is rice.

—

Heart & Soil supplements cover organ nutrition — no liver cooking needed.

!

Chicken is the worst fit for these macros — it needs about 70 g of added fat to land where a flap steak lands on 32 g. It's kept as a once-a-week slot for variety, not as a staple.

!

Romaine/cabbage salad is technically a "minimize" item under the strict animal-based hierarchy (plant defense compounds), but it's well-tolerated and kept in by preference.

!

These gram figures are standard reference values, not lab numbers for your specific beef — fat content varies batch to batch. They're accurate enough to hold a target within a few percent, which is all a body composition goal needs.
