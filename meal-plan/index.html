The kids — a different question

Mike and Janna's numbers come from a goal weight, because the point is to arrive somewhere else. Jameson, Calvin and Amelia are growing, so theirs come from the estimated energy requirement equations instead — the standard DRI formulas for children, at the 50th percentile for each age and an “active” activity level. Redo them if a real height and weight lands far off the median.

Their split is deliberately not the adults'. A 49%-fat day is a fat-loss design; the pediatric ranges want more carbohydrate and less fat, so the kids get proportionally more rice and less beef from the same three foods. The rings below show it: Mike sits at 49% fat, Jameson at 38%.

Three Meals, Weighed

The same three meals every day — no deciding, no rotating templates. Every number below is grams on the scale, and every meat weight is cooked weight, because that's what actually goes in the Tupperware. Weigh it once, and the day lands on target without any arithmetic.

01

Tare the container. Tupperware on the scale, press tare, then add food. The lid stays off.

02

Cooked weight, always. Meat and rice are weighed after cooking, out of the pan, not raw.

03

Sauce goes on the scale too. Pour the vinaigrette straight into the cup on the scale — it carries real fat, so eyeballing it is what drifts.

04

Close enough is close enough. Within 5 g is on target. Don't chase the last gram.

05

Weigh by density, not by habit. Honey is 82% carbs and butter is 81% fat — those get the scale every time. Lettuce is 17 kcal per 100 g; a handful is fine.

Breakfast and lunch are identical Monday through Friday, so this strip only tells you which meat to pull for dinner — and which day is the session. One Sunday cook builds Monday through Friday. Nothing sits in the fridge longer than four days, because Friday's boxes go to the freezer on Sunday and come down Thursday night.

Saturday and Sunday are off the grid on purpose. Wedding days, church and family are exactly where a seven-day plan breaks, and two guaranteed failures a week is what makes the other five feel pointless. Eat the fallback, log it roughly, and let five clean weekdays be the whole target.

The Egg Bake

Breakfast used to ask for five weighings per person before coffee. Across five people that would be twenty-five, at 6 a.m., before a workout, a shift and a school run. Same eggs, same beef, same butter, same macros — cooked as one bake on prep day and cut to weight. The ratio is 154 egg : 80 beef : 9 butter and it has never changed; only the size has.

Method. Whisk, fold in the weighed cooked beef and the melted butter, salt and garlic powder to taste. Divide evenly between two parchment-lined half sheet pans (13×18), about ¾ inch deep each. 350°F for 36 minutes, on two racks, swapping them at 18 — a home oven is never even top to bottom, and an unswapped pair gives you one weeping pan and one rubbery one.

Pull it early. An overbaked egg bake weeps water in the fridge and turns rubbery by Wednesday. Underdone-looking in the center is correct — carryover finishes it on the counter. The parchment is what lets you lift each slab out to weigh it.

Range oven, not the Wonder Oven — a 13×18 half sheet will not fit a countertop unit, let alone two. The Wonder Oven's job is the other end of the week; see The Daily Loop.

Cutting the slabs — no arithmetic required

The bake loses water in the oven, so a fixed gram cut would drift week to week. Instead: weigh both cooled slabs together (tare the parchment first), find that weight below, and read off five portion sizes. Each person gets five pieces — one per weekday. Round to the nearest row; rule 04 covers the gap.

Each portion is a fixed share of whatever the bake actually weighed — Mike 29.8%, Janna 22.7%, Jameson 18.0%, Calvin 16.8%, Amelia 12.6% — which is why shrinkage cannot push anyone off their macros.

The Session

There used to be two — Sunday and Wednesday, about four hours a week between them — and across ten days they produced zero compliant days. The problem was never discipline. Four hours of kitchen work a week on two named days, with both adults present, is a second job with a fixed schedule. So it is one Sunday session now, and Friday's portions go to the freezer instead of getting a second cook.

Feeding five costs about ten minutes more than feeding two. The long poles — the beef, the rice, the oven — barely scale with headcount. What scales is the portioning, and that now happens inside the baking window rather than after it: the boxes start at 1:10, not when the bake comes out. That single move is what absorbs three more people.

The Daily Loop

What's left once prep day has done its job. There is no microwave at home — a Wonder Oven on the counter, a range, a gas stovetop, and a TurboChef at the shop. That changes the clock on breakfast, not the argument for prepping it.

Breakfast now takes about twelve minutes instead of three. That sounds like it undoes the fix, and it doesn't — because the win was never speed, it was that none of those minutes are yours. Ten minutes of an oven running while you make coffee is a different kind of time than ten minutes standing at a stove cracking and weighing eggs. Still zero weighings, zero decisions, one tray to wash instead of a pan.

Order the morning around the oven. It's the long pole now, so it goes first: breakfast into the Wonder Oven before the kettle, before getting dressed, before anything else. Coffee gets made while it runs, and breakfast is hot when the coffee is. Those twelve minutes cost nothing, because you were awake and moving through them anyway.

Nothing plastic goes in the TurboChef. It moves air well north of 500°F and will warp or melt a meal-prep container. Janna tips the box onto a ceramic plate at the shop, every time. And if the shop's unit has a microwave element — most café models do, including the Sota, Bullet and i-series — then no foil and no metal either. Ceramic, full stop. Worth checking the badge on yours once and settling it.

Don't plan on eating the egg bake cold. This is the one place the fat-forward macros work against you — rendered 80/20 fat congeals in the fridge and goes waxy on the tongue. Room temperature is tolerable if you pull it out twenty minutes ahead; properly reheated is much better.

Start Here

Neither of us has strung a single compliant day together yet, so going straight to a five-person, twenty-five-box session is a low-probability bet. Stage it instead — each week adds one thing, and each week is a real win on its own.

And pick a tier every Saturday, out loud, with the calendar open. Full is the 2h26 session above. Half is the egg bake alone — 25 breakfasts, about 55 minutes, lunch is whatever the day gives you. Floor is nothing prepped, which is a chosen tier and not a lapse. There is no such thing as a failed week; there is a week you picked the wrong tier for.

Then put the Sunday block on the calendar as a real event, with both of you on it. This page already knew that unscheduled work does not happen — it is why the portioning moved inside the oven window — and then never once put the session itself on a calendar. That is the step that has never been taken.

Week 1
Breakfast only — the Half tier.One egg bake on Sunday, about 55 minutes, twenty-five portions. Lunch and dinner exactly as you were already eating them — genuinely, no changes, no guilt. The only goal is five consecutive school mornings that just happen: no cooking, no decisions, one honey weighing. That is the streak we have never had, and it is the hardest meal of the week to win.
Week 2
Add the lunch boxes — the Full tier.Now run the whole Sunday session, all twenty-five boxes. Dinner still unstructured. This is the week you find out how long the two passes actually take in this kitchen with five people's worth of containers on the counter — write the real number down and trust it over the one on this page.
Week 3
Add dinner and the swap table.The full plan. By now the sessions are routine and dinner is the only decision left in the day.
Week 4+
Adjust the numbers, not the system.Weigh in weekly, same day, same conditions. If the scale goes flat for three honest weeks, drop one rice tier — see If the scale stalls. Never change the system that is finally running.

When a day goes sideways

It will. The plan has to survive it, or the first bad Tuesday takes the whole month with it.

You skip breakfast entirelyEat the lunch box at lunch anyway. One missed meal is a 750-calorie day off target, not a failed week. Do not make it up at dinner — that is how the swap table stops meaning anything.
You're out and the box is at homeNearest steak or burger patty, no bun, no fries. Log it roughly. Move on.
Dinner plans changeGround beef fallback — 232 g Mike, 202 g Janna, no sauce. The day still lands.
The Sunday session doesn't happenDrop to the Half tier on Monday night — the egg bake only, about 55 minutes, and lunch is whatever the day gives you. Do not try to rescue the full session midweek; that is the move that used to end the whole thing. Half the system beats none of it.
A Thursday box smells offBin it. Four days is the ceiling and it isn't worth testing. Ground beef fallback for that meal.
One of you is out, the other isn'tKeep going alone. This is two independent stacks of boxes that happen to get built in the same session — it does not need both of you compliant to work for either of you.

Quick Tips

The plan didn't fail before because it was wrong. First it asked for ten weighings before coffee. Then, once that was fixed, it asked for four hours a week across two named days with both adults present — and produced zero compliant days in ten. These are the habits that keep it from asking.

The one that matters most: logging is the floor, and prep is a dial. Four saved foods is thirty seconds a day, but you could only tap them if the boxes existed — so one blown Sunday cost the prep and the part that was already free, and nothing ever accumulated. Log every day at every tier, including the ones where nothing was prepped.
